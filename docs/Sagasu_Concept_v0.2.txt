Sagasu — Concept Document

Version: 0.2 (working draft) Date: 2026-11-15 Status: Conceptual
discussion; no XML/ERD changes approved

1. Vision

Sagasu is an AI-assisted procurement platform that helps buyers
discover, evaluate, compare, and shortlist suppliers, collaborate using
organizational knowledge, and produce auditable bidder lists. The
initial prototype focuses on supplier discovery and bidder-list
creation; the longer-term vision includes approval governance and
agentic AI improvement.

2. Business problem

Supplier knowledge is fragmented across ERP master data, the open web,
purchasing history, individual buyers’ experience, evaluations, and
bidding records. Sagasu aims to improve discovery, knowledge
sharing, decision quality, and traceability while retaining human
responsibility for procurement decisions.

3. Main users

-   Buyers: search suppliers, create bidding projects, group items,
    review AI suggestions, comment, and select candidates.
-   Approvers and team managers: examine rationale, comments, and bidder
    lists; approve or request adjustments.
-   Audit department: reconstruct decisions, changes, supporting
    evidence, and who acted when.
-   AI agent: assists within defined permissions; does not independently
    establish adverse supplier facts.

4. Current functional concept

4.1 Supplier registry

Supplier identity, country, tax ID, ERP vendor code, and description;
later support for provenance and multiple data sources.

4.2 Bidding projects and bidder lists

Projects/RFQs contain items, quantities, and units. Buyers organize
items into editable groups and associate candidate suppliers with each
group. A consolidated bidder list is created for the project, with buyer
comments and approval status.

4.3 AI supplier suggestions

AI suggests suppliers for material groups, provides reasons, and
distinguishes its suggestions from buyer selections. Capture
source/evidence and user adjustments.

4.4 Peer collaboration

Buyers can share observations and reviews to improve team knowledge.
Comments are not all equally visible or suitable for future AI use;
access policies and attribution need design.

4.5 Approvals

Reusable approval strategies and ordered steps; record actual approvers,
outcomes, comments, and timestamps.

5. Principles agreed or strongly supported

1.  Auditable decisions: retain the original state and evidence
    available at the time.
2.  Human accountability: AI recommends; authorized people decide.
3.  Explainability: AI explains the factual basis for its conclusions
    without automatically exposing a comment author.
4.  Controlled oversight: authorized managers/auditors can trace
    restricted evidence and attribution, subject to explicit access
    policy and access logging.
5.  Learning from corrections: feedback can improve future
    recommendations, but an observation is not automatically a verified
    fact.
6.  Separation of history and current knowledge: preserve historical
    records even as validated knowledge changes.
7.  Privacy is not one Boolean: visibility, use in authorized AI
    context, and eligibility for future learning are separate
    dimensions.
8.  Learning permission does not equal permission to cite, quote, or
    disclose a source.

6. Privacy, collaboration, and evidence — design proposal

-   Visibility scope: authorized bid-list participants vs authorized
    broader team/organization (exact roles TBD).
-   AI context permission: may AI use the comment in a workflow where
    the requesting user is entitled to its contents?
-   AI learning permission: may it be considered as a candidate for
    future reusable knowledge?
-   Disclosure/citation policy: whether original text, identity, or
    derived conclusions may be shown to a particular audience.
-   Audit override is not unrestricted access: define
    lawful/organizational authorization, access logging, and controls.
-   Private-to-document comments may be eligible for restricted AI
    learning, but outputs must not leak their content through
    paraphrases or indirect identification.
-   If no disclosure-safe rationale can be generated, AI should provide
    a limited explanation or escalate to authorized review rather than
    reveal restricted facts.

7. Anti-blacklisting and evidence governance

A single buyer’s negative comment must not silently blacklist a
supplier. Proposed safeguards: - Store observations with provenance,
time, scope, and status (unverified/verified/disputed/superseded). -
Distinguish allegations, documented incidents, transactional evidence,
and formal evaluations. - Require corroboration or authorized review
before severe adverse assessments influence recommendations. - Allow
correction, challenge, and contextual updates; do not erase the audit
history. - Surface uncertainty and distinguish low confidence from
proven supplier performance. - Log who reviewed or approved a material
knowledge change.

8. Audit and revision design considerations

Retain: - Bid-list versions/snapshots, including group membership and
selected suppliers. - Change events (actor, action, before/after or
immutable references, timestamp, reason). - Approval step and actual
acting user. - AI execution ID, model/version, prompt/version, relevant
configuration, retrieved evidence identifiers/versions, output,
timestamp, and user request. - Access to sensitive audit evidence should
itself be logged. - Historical AI recommendations should remain
reconstructable as-of their original decision time; later knowledge must
not overwrite the past.

9. AI improvement architecture

Separate three layers: A. Immutable audit trail: what happened and what
was known then. B. Feedback and validation: buyer corrections, rejection
reasons, review decisions, and outcome signals. C. Governed knowledge
memory: validated, permission-aware facts usable in later
recommendations.

Recommended initial approach: retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) and
structured feedback rather than immediate model fine-tuning. A
correction is a learning candidate, not automatically ground truth.
Changes to ranking rules, prompts, or knowledge should be tested and
versioned.

10. Data sources

Current conceptual sources

-   Supplier master and ERP identifiers.
-   Public web and selected external supplier sources.
-   Buyer and approver comments/reviews.
-   Bid-project, material-group, bidder-list, and approval history.

Planned / future sources (not yet designed)

-   Purchase history: supplier, material, description, buyer, date, plus
    available transactional attributes.
-   Annual supplier evaluation: structured performance data,
    methodology, period, and evidence. These sources should remain
    separate in provenance and governance; no schema changes are
    approved for them yet.

11. Existing ERD baseline (13 tables)

gr_buyer, user, supplier, review, bid_project, bid_item, bid_list,
bid_itm_group, bid_group_item, suppliers_in_itm_group, approval_stg,
approval_step, approval_rcd.

Existing fields include review.is_private, review.ai_accessible,
approval_rcd.is_private, approval_rcd.ai_accessible; supplier assignment
includes ai_reason, source_type, is_ai_suggested, is_selected. These are
baseline fields, not final policy definitions.

13. Open design questions

-   What are the exact peer visibility levels and who can change them?
-   Are buyer identities visible to peers, anonymized in ordinary views,
    or always attributable to certain roles?
-   What evidence threshold should apply to a negative supplier
    assessment?
-   Who can validate, dispute, or retire AI knowledge?
-   Can a private comment inform aggregate metrics, and under what
    minimum-group and disclosure rules?
-   How should approval snapshots and revisions work when a list is
    returned for correction?
-   Which AI actions require mandatory human approval?
-   What retention periods, access logs, and deletion/legal-hold
    requirements apply?
-   How are confidential facts explained to users without revealing
    protected sources?

14. ForLater backlog

-   Passive behavioral feedback: observe when buyers remove, reorder, or
    ignore AI-suggested suppliers without explicit comments; treat as
    weak signals, not verified knowledge. Requires careful auditability
    and interpretation.
-   Purchase-history ingestion and linking to
    supplier/material/buyer/date.
-   Annual supplier evaluation integration.
-   More autonomous agentic workflows with human-controlled knowledge
    updates.
-   Model fine-tuning only if justified by validated data and
    governance.
-   Advanced supplier data provenance, freshness, and confidence.
-   Detailed role/permission matrix and evidence-disclosure policy.
-   Supplier dispute/correction process.
-   Metrics to evaluate AI suggestion quality and feedback
    effectiveness.

## 17. Decisions from follow-up discussion (v0.2)
- MVP default: buyer comments are shared with authorized buyers across the organization.
- MVP exception: buyers may restrict a comment to authorized participants of its bidder list.
- Buyer attribution is retained for audit and appropriate managerial oversight; normal AI explanations need not identify the buyer.
- AI explanations must show the grounds for recommendations without exposing restricted source text or identities. Where a safe explanation is impossible, limit the explanation and refer to authorized review.
- A single buyer's negative comment cannot silently become a supplier blacklist. Preserve evidence status, corroboration and human review for consequential adverse conclusions.
- The AI-learning flag means eligible for controlled validation and knowledge reuse, not immediate training, publication, or unquestioned factual acceptance.
- Visibility, AI use within authorized context, and AI learning eligibility are separate controls.

## 18. ForLater additions (v0.2)
- Configurable delayed publication: share comments after 60 days, after bidding award, or another company-defined trigger.
- Protect comments during an active tender or award process. The publication clock and triggers must account for cancelled, extended, rejected, or never-awarded processes.
- Confidentiality overrides must prevent automatic publication even when the time or award trigger is met.
- Future per-company policy settings for publication and exception approval.
- Purchase-history integration by supplier, material, description, buyer and date.
- Supplier annual evaluation integration, developed as a separate feature.
- Passive buyer-action learning (removing, reordering or ignoring suggestions), retained as a weak and auditable signal, not validated truth.

## 19. Proposed schema v3 mapping (design proposal)
Baseline 13 tables retained. Proposed additions: bid_list_version, audit_event, ai_run, ai_feedback, ai_knowledge, ai_knowledge_source (19 tables total).
- review and approval_rcd: new visibility_scope, ai_context_allowed, ai_learning_allowed. Existing is_private and ai_accessible retained for backward compatibility pending migration policy.
- suppliers_in_itm_group: optional ai_run_id and feedback_status for linking suggestions to an execution.
- Bid list version snapshots and append-only audit events enable historical reconstruction.
- AI runs preserve model, prompt, input/evidence references, output, timestamp and status.
- Feedback captures explicit buyer corrections and their validation status.
- Governed knowledge retains validation state, time validity, source provenance and permissions.
- Unique keys proposed for one list per project, group-supplier, strategy-sequence, version numbering and group-item membership.
- The XML is a logical design. Database-level NOT NULL constraints, foreign-key actions, authorization, audit immutability, and Rails migration details still require implementation decisions.
- No delayed-publication scheduler, purchase history tables, annual evaluation tables, or passive-learning mechanism is implemented in v3.
